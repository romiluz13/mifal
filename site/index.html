Task 1

Every agent gets a brief.

opus-cto → haiku-researcher
Now I need you to break my plan before I build it, because a wrong design costs minutes here and days in the code.

A brief says why the work matters, what done means, what the agent may decide alone and when to stop. Before it goes out, the validator checks it, and refuses one without its why, outcome, done means or stop rule.

  • Why
  • Outcome
  • Latitude
  • Pointers
  • Least sure of
  • Done means
  • Stop rule
  • Return

Every rule has a scar.

Retros change the lead itself. A rule that came from a failure carries the failure in a comment, so the next editor knows why it exists. Two from the team lead skill:

scar, ticket 7
A brief that asked for a push was obeyed over the reservation, which was written somewhere the worker did not read.
scar, ticket 5

The lead's shift manual.

The team lead skill is written like an engineering manager's playbook: ten moments in a shift, each with when it applies, what to ask, what to do and what to record.

  1. Taking the team

    Trust the record and the repo over recollection. Reconcile work in flight before dispatching anything.

  2. Assigning

    Could a strong engineer holding only this message do it right? Say the part you are least sure of.

  3. Waiting well

    Don't message a working teammate. Wait with a named limit.

  4. Blocked

    Approvals go to the human with a recommendation. Hold that teammate; keep the rest working.

  5. A report comes in

    A completion signal is not enough. Check the decisive claim at the revision named.

  6. Pushback

    Thank them first. Settle it on evidence, not rank, and say "I was wrong" when you were.

  7. Something broke

    Diagnose your brief, then the context, the tools and the service. The teammate comes last.

  8. Milestone and retro

    At most two changes, each with an owner and a check on the next task.

  9. Managing up

    No surprises: bad news early, with a recommendation.

  10. Yielding and closing

    A pause is not a final report. Record what is on disk and the first resume action.

Five agents. One floor.

Mifal ships 39 skills and offers Guide wiring for five agent families; only selected, eligible homes are wired, and live compatibility is limited to the recorded cases. The skills are Matt Pocock's engineering workflow run as ordered phases, plus DDD, review and the team lead. Herdr gives each agent its own pane, so the lead can see, brief and hear from any mix of them.

Claude Code~/.claude/CLAUDE.md
Codex~/.codex/AGENTS.md
Droid~/.factory/AGENTS.md
Pi~/.pi/agent/AGENTS.md
OpenCode~/.config/opencode/AGENTS.md
  • You keep the keys. Approvals, product choices and publishing stay yours. The lead brings them to you with a recommendation and keeps the rest of the team working.
  • Gates between phases. The Guide asks the lead to run record checks before every dispatch, review and acceptance. The validator checks the record (missing fields are refused and the fix is printed); a reviewer must still check that the claimed work and evidence are real.
  • One writer per file. Parallel work goes to isolated worktrees, and nothing is reviewed until every writer has returned.

Open the factory.

macOS, git and Python 3.9 or later. The dry run lists every write and changes nothing. If Herdr is missing, the installer runs brew install herdr, which the dry run shows first; without Homebrew, the installer downloads Herdr's script, shows its sha256 and asks before running it.

Then start your agents inside Herdr, and tell one of them to lead the team toward your goal.

  • Alpha. The installer, update, uninstall, doctor and validator are covered by tests, run in CI on macOS under the system Python and the current python3. Recorded one-Mac workflows cover Claude Code, Codex (its codex-work home), Droid, Pi and OpenCode, with the limits the support section in the README states; it was recorded on the 0.2.4 code, and this release's wiring for Codex and OpenCode has not been rechecked live. The team lead's Herdr loop is experimental: it is tested with fake agents, imports in an installed copy since this release, and has not been run end to end there.
  • Boundaries. macOS only this release. There is no sandbox or isolation between your agents, and recovery from a wedged worker is one budgeted resume action: the lead, not a watchdog, notices a worker gone quiet.
  • Reversible. mifal uninstall removes unchanged managed effects and keeps edits and additions; occupied backups and Herdr may remain. It does not restore a home byte for byte.
